# Calibration, not ban lists, gives agents taste

An AI coding agent produces generic UI because generic UI is literally its most probable output: pretraining makes the Tailwind/shadcn/Inter/indigo look the mode of web code, preference tuning sharpens that mode, and every gap in a brief gets filled from it. Banning the mode does not fix this; it moves the agent to the next mode (Space Grotesk, then cream + serif + terracotta, then whatever comes after). The measurement taken in this session shows why ban lists aim at the wrong variable: linear.app uses Inter, a monospace face, 81 pill-shaped elements, 58 gradients and 136 bordered elements, which is a checklist of "AI tells", and it still reads as tasteful, because every value is *calibrated*. It uses four near-black grounds one or two RGB steps apart, four ink greys carrying all the hierarchy, a custom 510 weight, −0.022em headline tracking and a spacing histogram packed onto five values. Your installed skills fail mostly for structural reasons, not because your prompts are bad. Fourteen of them compete for the same request and contradict each other on the decisions that set the look. The most data-driven one lost to no skill in its own blind tests. The keyword-to-style one returns the slop palette verbatim. Emil Kowalski's was never about art direction. What does work, on the evidence, is to give the agent numbers it cannot get from its prior: a subject-grounded brief, references converted into measured tokens rather than pasted as screenshots, a written token plan before code, a deterministic lint for code-certain defects, and one or two rounds of screenshot critique by a separate, rubric-driven critic that keeps the best version. That fits into one lean skill, and the recipe is at the end. You do not lack taste. You are the only judge in this loop whose sense of "distinctive" is reliable, and the system should be built around that.

Most of what the design world "knows" about taste is expert heuristic, not experiment, and the table says so.

## Slop is the model's mode, sharpened by tuning and refilled by every gap in the brief

The generic look is not a bug in a particular model; it is what a well-trained model is supposed to produce when nothing in the context pushes it elsewhere. Anthropic names it **"distributional convergence"**: without direction, Claude's landing pages drift to Inter, purple gradients on white and minimal motion because "safe design choices" dominate the training data and the model samples the "high-probability center" **[vendor]** ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). The research explains why tuning makes it worse rather than better. Kirk et al. found RLHF **"substantially" lowers output diversity** compared with supervised fine-tuning, most strongly within a single prompt, even when sampling at temperature 1 **[measured]** ([arXiv 2310.06452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452)). Zhang et al. trace part of it to raters: annotators preferred the more typical, higher-likelihood response **51.6–60.8% of the time** at equal correctness, and optimizing against that reward is mathematically a sharpening of the base distribution (an exponent γ > 1 on the base model), which concentrates mass on the most stereotypical completion **[measured + theory]** ([arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)). The "Artificial Hivemind" study adds that different models converge on strikingly similar answers to open-ended prompts, with the inter-model effect the stronger one **[measured]** ([arXiv 2510.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)). That is why switching from Claude to GPT or Gemini rarely escapes the look. No study has yet measured this for UI specifically; applying it to UI is **[inference]**, though a consistent one.

The specific ingredients have mundane origins. The widely repeated story that indigo comes from Tailwind UI's `bg-indigo-500` default spreading through starters and tutorials is plausible, and Tailwind's creator half-jokingly apologized for it, but it has never been verified against training data **[anecdote]** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)). Some builder system prompts actively order the look. Google Antigravity's prompt demands "vibrant colors, dark modes, glassmorphism", "smooth gradients" and Inter/Roboto/Outfit, and says that failing to "WOW the user" is "UNACCEPTABLE" ([prompt mirror](https://github.com/x1xhlol/system-prompts-and-models-of-ai-tools/blob/main/Google/Antigravity/Fast%20Prompt.txt)). v0 goes the other way: exactly 3–5 colours, no purple, no gradients, no decorative blobs, and a mandatory design-inspiration step before any code ([prompt mirror](https://github.com/x1xhlol/system-prompts-and-models-of-ai-tools/blob/main/v0%20Prompts%20and%20Tools/Prompt.txt)).

The most important mechanism for anyone writing a skill is what happens when you ban the mode. Anthropic's own post warned that after banning Inter, Claude "still tend[s] to converge on common choices (Space Grotesk, for example)" ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). A year later the community catalogue has a full **second tier of tells** that appeared once tier 1 was banned: warm cream (~#F4F1EA) with a high-contrast serif and a terracotta accent (~#D97757, which Anthropic's current skill identifies as "Anthropic's own Claude-interaction accent"), near-black with one acid-green accent, broadsheet hairlines, tracked all-caps eyebrows, mono data labels and '→' on every link ([anthropics/skills SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md); [avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)). Claude Design's own guidance, quoted second-hand, says the model has "a consistent default house style" that is "persistent", and that "don't use cream" shifts it "to a different fixed palette rather than producing variety" ([Generative Labs](https://www.generativelabs.com/insights/why-ai-design-tools-look-the-same)). There is also a mechanistic reason bans backfire at the token level. Across 40,000 samples, the chance of violating "do not say X" rose with how natural X was, and **87.5% of violations were priming failures**: naming the forbidden thing activated it **[measured, single-author preprint]** ([arXiv 2601.08070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08070)).

This answers the second half of Q3. A uniquely designed site differs from the mode because it contains information the mode doesn't have: the subject's own material (its photographs, vocabulary, data, history) and a designer's specific numeric decisions. Shin et al.'s qualitative review of vibe-coding tools names **"frictionless generation"** as the driver of homogenization: creators accept defaults because nothing asks them to decide. They propose "productive friction", where the tool surfaces design assumptions and alternatives before committing ([arXiv 2603.13036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13036)). A practitioner guide to Google Stitch says the same thing bluntly: any category the prompt omits, "the AI fills in … with common defaults" **[anecdote]** ([DEV](https://dev.to/seifalmotaz/stop-generating-ai-slop-the-developers-guide-to-google-stitch-jen)).

## Linear ships six banned tells and still reads as expensive

If tells caused ugliness, linear.app would look like slop. A computed-style probe run in this session at a 1024px viewport says otherwise **[measured here]** ([linear.app](https://linear.app), probe of computed styles).

| Dimension | What linear.app ships | Which "rule" or tell it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Type families | Inter Variable + Berkeley Mono | Inter is tier-1 slop for Anthropic and Impeccable; mono outside code is banned by taste-skill and a tell for frontend-design |
| Shape | 9999px radius on **81 elements** | Pill badges/eyebrow chips are on every tells list |
| Surface | **58 gradient backgrounds**, **136 bordered elements** | Gradients and hairline boxes are tells; taste-skill bans 1px outlined boxes |
| Grounds | rgb(8,9,10), rgb(15,16,17), rgb(16,17,18), rgb(22,23,24), plus white at **1–2% alpha** | Tinted near-black instead of #000 is a frontend-design tier-2 tell |
| Ink | 4 greys: rgb(247,248,248), (208,214,224), (138,143,152), (98,102,109) | NN/g suggests ≤3 contrast variations |
| Weight | **510** (custom, variable axis) | Not on any scale an LLM emits by default |
| Headline | **56px, line-height 1.1, tracking −0.022em** | Only 5.5vw, nearer the "weak site" median than the "top site" one in taste-skill's study |
| Body | **13–15px** | Below Butterick's 15px floor and v0's "no text under 14px" |
| Spacing | Concentrated on **4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 32px** | 6px is off the 4px grid |
| Ratio | Headline/body **3.7** | Matches Vignelli's "decisive, ≥2×" contrast |

What makes it work is visible in the numbers once you look for relationships instead of ingredients **[inference]**. The four grounds differ by only one to seven RGB levels; the darkest-to-lightest contrast is about 1.11:1 by my calculation from the probe values. Elevation is expressed as **tone steps rather than borders or shadows**, and the "borders" that do exist are white at 1–2% opacity, barely separators at all. That is exactly what Linear says it built: a theme system reduced from **98 hand-defined variables to 3 inputs** (base, accent, contrast), generated in perceptually uniform LCH, with elevation as lightness steps and the accent's influence "deliberately limited" for a more timeless look ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). The four ink greys do the hierarchy work that size does on most AI pages. By the WCAG formula they sit at roughly 18.7, 13.6, 6.1 and 3.5:1 on the darkest ground, a clean descending staircase, which is Refactoring UI's "emphasize by de-emphasizing" made literal ([book summary](https://sglavoie-github-io.pages.dev/posts/2023/09/09/book-summary-refactoring-ui/)). The weakest grey would fail WCAG AA if it sets small text; the probe did not record which elements use it, so that is a flag for lint and a judgement call, not a verdict. The headline settings (tight leading, negative tracking) follow the typographic consensus that large type wants less line-height and less letter-spacing ([visual foundations, Refactoring UI summary](https://sglavoie-github-io.pages.dev/posts/2023/09/09/book-summary-refactoring-ui/)). The spacing histogram shows a decisive jump from 12px to 32px: small gaps inside groups and big gaps between them. That is the Gestalt proximity rule, "more whitespace between groups than within them", applied with a ratio of about 2.7 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/)).

Two lessons follow for Q1 and Q5. First, **taste lives in calibrated relationships** (ratios between steps, how few values are used, where the single jump happens), and these are invisible to a ban list that only checks whether an ingredient is present. "Pill radius" is not a tell; an undifferentiated pill on every element with one default shadow is. Taste-skill's own gallery study is consistent with this. Top-scored sites used gradients on **22%** vs **52%** of weak ones, not 0% ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)). Second, the published practice of top teams converges on **constraint by construction**. Linear derives everything from three inputs. Stripe aligned every hue to the same lightness curve so that **any two colours five or more steps apart pass body-text contrast** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems)). Vercel prescribes layered shadows, semi-transparent borders, concentric radii and hue-tinted neutrals ([Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). The canonical sources reduce to a restraint budget that a skill can state as defaults: at most 2 families, 3 weights, 3 content sizes, 3 text inks, 1–2 accent hues and one primary action per group (NN/g, Refactoring UI, Vignelli and Butterick; the exact caps are expert heuristics with no controlled evidence behind them) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/); [Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)). Linear's four inks show the budget is a default to be overridden by a measured reference, not a law.

"Intuitive" is a separate, better-evidenced layer. Nielsen's ten heuristics translate almost one-to-one into code checks: pending, error and success states on every action; Esc and cancel on every overlay; undo or confirm for destructive actions; specific error messages ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)). WCAG 2.2 sets a **24×24 CSS px** minimum target ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Response feels instant under **0.1 s**, needs a busy indicator past **1 s**, and needs determinate progress with cancel past **10 s** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). The craft writers add the details that make a product feel finished: no animation on high-frequency or keyboard-initiated actions, ease-out under about 300ms, never scale from 0, origin-aware popovers, a reduced-motion fallback ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations); [Rauno Freiberg](https://github.com/raunofreiberg/interfaces)). Real content beats lorem ipsum because placeholder text hides wrapping and truncation failures (practitioner consensus, no primary study) ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/should-we-use-lorem-ipsum-in-product-design-f1a09b9fd3ec)). AI output usually renders only the happy, medium-density state, which is a low-craft signal a lint can catch **[inference]**.

On Q2, the evidence on Apple is thinner than its reputation, and the HIG pages could not be fetched. Apple frames its platform principles as **hierarchy, harmony and consistency**: put the most important content in focus, be judicious with colour in controls so they recede, and keep standard icons and predictable action placement ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/liquid-glass.md)). Rauno Freiberg's essay explains what makes it feel physical: dismissals carry the throw's momentum, destructive gestures commit only on release, motion is interruptible mid-flight, elements return to where they came from, and frequent actions lose their animation ([rauno.me](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). Linear explicitly borrowed Apple's spacing conventions so its app "feels native" ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). The best reading **[inference]** is that Apple stands out because one organization owns the whole stack and treats behaviour as the design, so its visual restraint is backed by interaction quality most web teams never build. It is not infallible: Liquid Glass drew legibility and distraction criticism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_Glass)). For a web agent, the transferable part is the behaviour (feedback, physics, consistency), which is exactly the part an image of an Apple page cannot transmit.

## Your installed skills cancel each other out

The local audit explains most of the "it works for others, not for me" experience without assuming anything about your prompts **[measured here]** ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)). **Fourteen installed skills match "build a landing page"**, including three copies of frontend-design and two each of taste-skill and ui-ux-pro-max. The model loads one or two, and which one is close to random from your side. Their instructions contradict each other on the axes that set the look. On hairline borders, taste-skill bans them while ux-ui's design-doctrine requires full hairline borders. On off-black on warm-white, design-doctrine requires it while frontend-design calls it a tell. Taste-skill requires a staggered entrance on every section and frontend-design calls that an AI giveaway. ui-ux-pro-max recommends JetBrains Mono and glassmorphism, which the others ban. Loaded across sessions, these swing between looks. Loaded together, they average into what you experience as "different but ugly". With two design skills loaded you carry roughly **20k tokens of partly conflicting instructions** on top of the task.

Each skill also fails on its own terms. **Taste-skill**, the richest and most data-driven, ran blind A/B tests against no skill at all, and **the no-skill baseline scored higher in 4 of 5 rounds** (skill 6.6 vs baseline 7.7 in round 1). It won only in round 5, on briefs it had already seen, with model judges. Its 10-step, ~10k-token pipeline also tends to lose steps 8 and 9 ("Look", "Move") in long sessions, which the skill itself warns about ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)). Its lesson, *removing ornament without adding substance makes the page worse*, is the most important finding in the whole ecosystem. **ui-ux-pro-max**, given "developer tool API monitoring minimal", returned Tailwind's default `slate-900/800/400/700` with `green-500`, JetBrains Mono and a dark + green accent, which is cluster #2 on frontend-design's list of AI tells. A keyword-to-style catalogue returns the category average by construction. Its documented script path also resolves to nothing in your `~/.claude/skills` copy, so it falls back to "built-in defaults". **Emil Kowalski's skill** is about 90% motion, easing, springs and component feel ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)). It makes an existing design feel good and says almost nothing about composition, type or colour, so it never touched the problem you have.

The wider ecosystem shows the same pattern. Practitioners on Hacker News who had tried Impeccable, ui-ux-pro-max and others said the skills fix inconsistencies and accessibility but "design converges on the same thing", and that one skill's showcase screenshots "are practically identical" to default Sonnet output **[anecdote]** ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49058547)). None of the popular skills publishes a controlled evaluation. Impeccable (~95k stars) and taste-skill (~105k stars) offer demos and testimonials ([Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable); [taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill)), and Anthropic's own post offers only captioned before/after screenshots ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). The instruction-following literature explains why long skills degrade quietly. On IFScale, accuracy falls as simultaneous instructions grow, with **Claude Opus 4 at 44.6% and Sonnet 4 at 42.9% at 500 instructions**. Failures are mostly **omissions** rather than wrong edits, and later instructions are dropped more than earlier ones **[measured]** ([arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)). Chroma found performance degrades with input length across 18 models, and similar-but-irrelevant distractors make it worse **[measured]** ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). An omitted taste rule falls back to the default, which is the slop, so a 500-line rule list is partly invisible exactly where it matters **[inference]**.

Q6 resolves into a variance story **[inference]**. A skill raises the floor, authors show their best draws, and the users who report "night and day" results tend to supply what the skill cannot invent. One practitioner's best result came from sketching by hand, redrawing in Figma and prompting with that image, though "LLMs still put something AI-ish into design anyway" **[anecdote]** ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49058547)). Another found project-specific generated images were the cheapest way out of the template look **[anecdote]** ([DEV, Jun Seo](https://dev.to/kkk_dev_1b0a00f5047cb4de6/why-every-claude-code-built-site-looks-the-same-and-the-image-layer-that-breaks-it-37jp)). With proper tooling, generated UIs were judged at least comparable to human-designed sites in **50% of cases** **[measured]** ([arXiv 2604.09577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09577)), so the ceiling is real, but reaching it depends on inputs and process.

## A screenshot carries the vibe but not the numbers

Q4 and Q8 share one diagnosis: **a reference image is a weak conditioning signal for the values that carry taste.** Design2Code tested GPT-4o, Gemini and Claude on 484 real webpages, and the models "mostly lag in recalling visual elements from the input webpages" and struggle with layout **[measured]** ([arXiv 2403.03163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03163)). Now look at what Linear's taste consists of: a 510 weight versus 500, −0.022em tracking, borders at 1–2% alpha, grounds one RGB step apart. None of that survives a compressed screenshot read by a vision model that tends to round towards familiar values **[inference]**. Where the image is silent or ambiguous, the sharpened prior fills in the gap, so you get "Linear-ish" with Tailwind defaults underneath. A good prompt cannot repair this, because the missing information is numeric. Bad sequencing usually adds to it: the reference arrives along with a dozen other instructions (and maybe two skills). The model writes code in one pass and never puts its render next to the reference. When it does look, it judges its own work. Refinement without a visual critique step improved web output by only **+1.5%**, against **+4.9%** for one cycle with a visual critic **[measured]** ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)).

The measured literature supports references for **fidelity and consistency**, not yet for taste. Retrieval of brand style references "significantly improves the consistency" of generated UI code (measured as CSS recall), with effectiveness that "heavily depends on the quality of the input data" ([doi 10.3390/engproc2025120037](https://doi.org/10.3390/engproc2025120037)). Design2Code's self-revision prompt feeds the model both the reference screenshot and its own render ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)). No controlled study compares brief-only, brief + image and brief + tokens on human-rated distinctiveness, which is the experiment this repo should run. The practical fix follows anyway **[inference]**: treat a reference as *direction* (palette, type, density, composition), and convert it into numbers before generation. If it is a live URL, run the same computed-style probe used on Linear and give the agent the resulting profile (grounds, ink steps, families and weights, size and tracking scale, radii and spacing histograms, border alphas). If it is an image, have the agent write down its estimates explicitly so you can correct them. Then compare renders side by side at the same width.

For Q7, the levers rank by evidence. **Personas** ("you are a senior Swiss designer") showed no accuracy benefit across 162 roles and four model families, and expert personas gave no consistent gain on hard benchmarks **[measured, on factual tasks, not aesthetics]** ([arXiv 2311.10054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054v2); [arXiv 2512.05858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05858)). **Negative instructions** prime what they forbid (above). **Long rule lists** get omitted. The best-measured diversity technique is **Verbalized Sampling**: ask for k candidates *with their probabilities*, optionally restricted to low-probability options, then pick. It produced **1.6–2.1× more diversity** in creative writing with no loss of accuracy, recovered 66.8% of base-model diversity, and gained more on stronger models **[measured on text, untested on UI]** ([arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)). Anthropic's context-engineering guidance points the same way: "diverse, canonical examples" over a "laundry list" of rules, and the "smallest possible set of high-signal tokens", at the "right altitude" between hex codes and vague mood words **[vendor]** ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). So the answer to "am I prompting wrong?" is mostly no. You are missing *context* (subject, real content, measured references) and *process* (plan before code, look after), and no rewording supplies either.

## Taste is trainable, and you are the judge the loop cannot replace

On Q10, nothing in the evidence suggests the problem is you. The mechanisms above produce generic output for everyone, and the strongest community voices report the same failure with the same skills. Two findings define your role. First, AI assistance raises individual quality while **homogenizing output across people**: AI-assisted stories were rated better but were more similar to each other, with the biggest lift for less creative writers **[measured]** ([Doshi & Hauser, Science Advances](https://datadryad.org/stash/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.qfttdz0pm)). Only the feedback-tuned model, not the base model, reduced essay diversity **[measured]** ([arXiv 2309.05196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196)). Second, reward models and LLM judges are **least calibrated to humans exactly where preferences are idiosyncratic** ([arXiv 2510.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)), and no study measures LLM-judge agreement on distinctiveness or "AI-lookingness" ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)). An automated critic can catch broken hierarchy and spacing; deciding whether a page is *yours* and not the mode stays with you.

Taste is also more trainable than it feels **[inference]**. The Linear probe is a training exercise in itself. Seeing that "expensive dark UI" decomposes into four grounds 1.11:1 apart, a four-step ink staircase and a 12-to-32px spacing jump turns a vague impression into a reusable judgement. Measuring five or ten sites you admire, and writing one sentence on why each choice works, is the designer's apprenticeship compressed. Expert critique examples transfer to models too: few-shot prompting with designer critiques from UICrit improved LLM UI feedback by about **55%** **[measured]** ([arXiv 2407.08850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850)). Your reference notes, written in your words, are the highest-value file a taste skill can load.

The steps for Q9 are the same whether a human or an agent follows them, and they mirror what every serious tool now externalizes before code. Impeccable uses PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md, ui-ux-pro-max a MASTER.md, taste-skill dealt directions, v0 a mandatory inspiration call, and Anthropic's current skill a token plan reviewed against "what would I produce for any similar prompt" ([anthropics/skills SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md); [Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)). Anthropic's skill moving from a ban list (2025) to calibration plus process (2026-06, 2026-09 rewrites) is implicit vendor evidence that the ban approach failed ([commit history](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/commits/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)).

## Recipe: one lean taste skill

The recipe below is the synthesis. Each parameter cites the evidence that sets it. Where the evidence is only inference, it says so, and the whole skill should be validated by blind human pairwise comparison against no skill, because taste-skill's own record shows that an untested skill can lose to baseline.

### Routing comes first: one voice

Disable or rename the descriptions of the competing art-direction skills (frontend-design copies, taste-skill, ui-ux-pro-max, design-screen, superdesign, design, ui-styling) so that only `taste` matches page and screen requests. Keep Emil Kowalski's motion skills (`animate`, `review-animations`) for motion polish, and invoke them only after the layout is settled. Rationale: the 14-way collision and the contradiction table ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)) and context-rot distractor effects ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)) **[inference from measured-here and measured]**.

### The process, in the order the core states it

The core SKILL.md lists the load-bearing steps first and keeps them short, because primacy bias and omission errors hit late, long instruction lists hardest ([arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538); [arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)).

1. **Ground the brief.** Name the subject, the audience, and the page's job: persuade, operate, read or experience (Impeccable's framing, [impeccable.style](https://impeccable.style)). Collect real content. If the brief has no subject-specific material, ask at most two questions, Shin et al.'s "productive friction" ([arXiv 2603.13036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13036)).
2. **Measure a reference.** For each reference URL, run `probe` and save the profile to `design/refs/*.json`. With no reference, load one from the curated profile library (measured sites, not named styles). For images, write explicit estimates for the user to correct. This step is the direct remedy for Design2Code's recall gap **[inference]**.
3. **Diverge, then drop the mode.** Write 5 candidate directions with verbalized probabilities and discard the two most probable. State the survivor as an idea sentence ("on this page, [something only this subject has] becomes [the device]") and apply taste-skill's competitor-swap test: if the sentence still works with a competitor's name in it, it is generic ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md); VS from [arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)).
4. **Write the token plan before code** into `DESIGN.md`: grounds, ink steps, accent, families and weights, a type scale with tracking and leading, a spacing set, radii, and the one place where boldness is spent. Specify everything positively, with numbers. Check the plan against `design/history.md` (past builds) so it doesn't repeat itself. Sources: Anthropic's plan → review → build ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)); Hallmark's build log ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49058547)).
5. **Build with real content and every state**: loading, empty-first-use, empty-filtered, error, sparse, dense ([NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)).
6. **Lint**, deterministically, and fix until clean.
7. **Look**, in the critique loop below.
8. **Hand over two finalists** and let the user choose. Append the decision to `design/history.md`.

### What lives in the core vs references

| Layer | Contents | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| **Core SKILL.md** | The 8 steps; the restraint defaults (≤2 families, ≤3 weights, ≤3 content sizes, ≤3–4 inks, 1–2 accent hues, one primary action per group, spacing inside groups well below spacing between them), each overridable by a measured reference profile; the rule "tells are smells to justify against the reference, not bans"; critique loop parameters | ~1.5–2k tokens. Anthropic's original was ~400 tokens and its current skill is ~1,500 words ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)) |
| **references/tells.md** | Tier-1 and tier-2 tell catalogue, each paired with its *replacement move*, loaded only for critique | On demand. Pairing every ban with a replacement avoids the priming failure ([arXiv 2601.08070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08070)) |
| **references/profiles/** | Measured reference profiles: Linear now, plus 5–10 sites the user admires, each with a one-paragraph "why it works" in the user's words | On demand, 1–3 per task (few relevant examples over big libraries) |
| **references/craft.md** | Nielsen/Vercel/Rauno rules for app screens, the states matrix, copy rules | On demand, for app screens |
| **scripts/** | `probe.mjs` (computed-style extraction), `lint.mjs`, `shoot.mjs` (screenshots at 375/1024/1440 plus the reference at the same widths) | Code, not context |

No style catalogue ships, because catalogues return category averages, as ui-ux-pro-max showed ([local skill audit](../research/01-skill-audit.md)).

### Lint vs judgement

Anything code-certain goes to the linter, so the visual critic's limited attention goes to composition. Avoid-ai-design's author draws the same line: scanners catch code-certain tells, while palette weight, spacing and hierarchy "need a render" ([avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)).

| Lint (deterministic, blocks) | Judgement (rendered, critic + human) |
|---|---|
| All colours, sizes and spaces resolve to tokens; no arbitrary `[13px]` literals | One unambiguous focal point per viewport (10px and 20px blur test, [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)) |
| Distinct families ≤2, weights ≤3, content sizes ≤3 (warn, reference may override) | Grouping reads correctly; nothing boxed that space already separates ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/)) |
| WCAG 2.2 contrast per text node (4.5/3:1) and 3:1 non-text ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)) | Calibration match against the reference profile (ink staircase, tone steps, spacing jump) |
| Targets ≥24px; prose ≤~65–75ch; body line-height in range | Idea sentence visibly realized; competitor-swap test |
| No `transition: all`, no `scale(0)`, UI durations ≤300ms, reduced-motion branch ([Emil](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)) | Tells present *without* a justification in DESIGN.md |
| `focus-visible` present; icon buttons labelled; `<a>` for navigation | Copy specificity: verb + noun CTAs, no vague headline ([Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/button)) |
| No lorem/"John Doe"/"Item 1"; `…` and `tabular-nums`; `<img>` dimensions | Boldness spent in one place, and the rest recessive |
| Every data component has empty/loading/error branches | Whether it is distinctive and *yours*: the user only |

### Critique loop parameters

| Parameter | Setting | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rounds | 1 by default, 2 if round 1 found a P0 issue, hard cap 3 | Gains flatten after cycle 1 (Sonnet 4.5: +4.9% at cycle 1, +6.1% at cycle 3) while tokens grow from ~2.6k to ~45.8k ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)); untrained models degrade after 2–3 rounds ([arXiv 2510.11498](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11498v1)) **[measured]** |
| Visual input | Screenshots at 375/1024/1440 with the reference beside each | Refinement without visual critique: +1.5% only ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)) **[measured]**; widths follow taste-skill **[vendor]** |
| Critic | Separate subagent with fresh context and no authorship | Separate critic models reduce self-bias ([arXiv 2412.16829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16829)) **[measured]** |
| Format | Multi-dimensional rubric (the judgement column above), **pairwise** against the best-so-far and the reference, never "rate 1–10" | Multi-dimensional judge 69.5% vs single-dimension 48.5% human agreement ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)); pairwise ~70–79% vs much weaker absolute scoring ([arXiv 2402.04788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04788)) **[measured]** |
| Few-shot | 2–3 example critiques written by the user in the critic prompt | Expert-critique few-shot: ~55% better UI feedback ([arXiv 2407.08850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850)) **[measured]** |
| Fixes per round | Top 1–3 issues, with exactly one prioritized target that the others must not break | Visual repair coupling; one prioritized target per round beats naive self-evolution ([arXiv 2608.24138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24138)) **[measured]** |
| Acceptance | Keep a revision only if it strictly beats the best so far; ship the **best**, not the last | Best-of-cycles +10.8% vs +6.1% final cycle ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)); forced optimization ~+2 pts ([arXiv 2510.11498](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11498v1)) **[measured]** |
| Order | Lint green before any visual round | Keeps critic budget on composition **[inference]** |

All these gains were scored by VLM judges, not designers, and none of the studies measured distinctiveness. The loop reliably improves *quality*; only references, the plan and your choice move *distinctiveness* **[inference]**.

## Conclusion

The standard framing, that AI lacks taste and ban lists teach it some, gets the problem backwards. The model has a strong, consistent taste: the sharpened average of the web. Ingredient bans only redirect it to the next average, as the Inter → Space Grotesk → cream-and-terracotta progression shows. The Linear measurement shows that what separates admired interfaces from slop is mostly invisible to a list of ingredients and very visible to a list of numbers: how close the tone steps are, how many inks there are, where the one big spacing jump sits, a weight of 510 instead of 500. A taste skill should therefore move information *into* the context (measured references, subject material, a written token plan, the user's own critiques) rather than ask the model to suppress what it already knows, and it should spend its verification budget on looking rather than on rules.

Two things remain open, and this repo is positioned to settle them. No one has run the controlled comparison of brief-only, brief + screenshot and brief + measured profile on human-rated distinctiveness, and Verbalized Sampling has never been tested on UI. Both experiments are cheap with the probe and the critique loop described here. Until they are run, the most defensible claim is the narrow one: the agent can reach the ceiling, but only when someone with taste supplies the numbers and makes the final call, and that person is you.
